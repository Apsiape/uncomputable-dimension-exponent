\section{The price of perfection}\label{sec:intro}
Which numbers can be the entanglement price of perfection for one fixed finite game? The positive dimension exponents of finite rational Boolean constraint-system (BCS) games of quantum value one are exactly the positive $\Delta^0_2$ reals. Every number in that class also occurs as the logarithmic coefficient of one-way distillable entanglement on the original mixed strategy state. The exponent measures how rapidly the least local dimension grows when one fixed device must play closer to perfection. It is the entanglement budget that precision forces on a device whose rules are fixed, so the class of possible exponents decides whether that budget can be read off from the finite rulebook. In general it cannot: a computable finite rulebook can have a noncomputable limiting resource rate. At the power scale, the positive exponents in $(0,1]$ of log dimension, pure entropy and formation are also exactly the $\Delta^0_2$ reals, with every such exponent realized for the original mixed-state usable demand. The full usable spectra remain open. The profile part classifies the positive finite lower/upper logarithmic dimension pairs and the power pairs in $(0,1]$, with matched original-state usable realizations. It also separates endpoint classification from realization of a whole precision profile.

A real is \emph{left-computably-enumerable}, or \emph{left-c.e.}, if it is the limit of a computable nondecreasing sequence of rationals. Increasing approximations below are understood in this non-strict sense. All logarithms without a displayed base are base two, and entanglement is measured in ebits. For a finite game $G$ of quantum value one, let $D_G(\eps)$ be the least local dimension of a strategy with loss at most $\eps$, padding the two local spaces equally. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:usable-definition}
 d^\to_G(\eps)=\inf_{\text{finite-dimensional strategies of loss }\le\eps}
 E_D^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB}).
\end{equation}
The state may be mixed. Its one-way distillable entanglement is the asymptotic Bell-pair rate using local operations and classical communication from Alice to Bob. A real is \emph{right-c.e.} if its negative is left-c.e.; it is $\Delta^0_2$ if it is the limit of a computable sequence of rationals, equivalently, by Shoenfield's limit lemma~\cite{Sho59}, if it is computable relative to the halting set. Let $\mathcal B_{\rm BCS}$ be the set of positive limits $\lim_{\eps\downarrow0}\log_2D_G(\eps)/\log_2(1/\eps)$ for finite BCS games with rational question probabilities and quantum value one.

\begin{theorem}[Complete dimension classification and matched realization]\label{thm:realization}
The positive dimension spectrum is
\[
 \mathcal B_{\rm BCS}=\{\alpha>0:\alpha\text{ is }\Delta^0_2\}.
\]
For every positive $\Delta^0_2$ real $\alpha$ there is a fixed finite two-player Boolean constraint-system game $G_\alpha$, with rational question probabilities, quantum value one and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, such that, as $\eps\downarrow0$,
\begin{align}
 \log_2D_{G_\alpha}(\eps)&=\alpha\log_2(1/\eps)+o(\log(1/\eps)),\label{eq:realization-dimension}\\
 d^\to_{G_\alpha}(\eps)&=\alpha\log_2(1/\eps)+o(\log(1/\eps)).\label{eq:realization-usable}
\end{align}
Conversely, every finite dimension exponent of a rational finite game is $\Delta^0_2$ whenever its defining limit exists. The usable-entanglement assertion is an inclusion, not a classification of its full spectrum.
\end{theorem}
\medskip\noindent\textit{Proof routes.} Our main proof is Corollary~\ref{cor:profile-no-witness}: a carry checker and programmed pulse counts establish the classification with no $\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}$ witness. Section~\ref{sec:classification} gives a second, witness-based proof and additionally supplies the projection-minimum and exact-trace results.


Corollary~\ref{cor:profile-no-witness} proves the realization, with a second proof in Section~\ref{sec:classification}; Lemma~\ref{lem:computability}, with the limit argument in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:complete-spectrum}, gives the converse (compare~\cite[Proposition~11.13]{DM-precision}). Corollary~\ref{cor:heat} extends the order contrast to every positive $\Delta^0_2$ coefficient: local-reset heat has coefficient $2\alpha$, while joint-reset heat stays bounded. This identifies limiting coefficients, without asserting computability or noncomputability of the finite-precision heat function.

\subsection{The halting game}
Fix a standard effective enumeration of programs, with halting set $K\subseteq\N=\{0,1,\ldots\}$.
\begin{theorem}[The halting game]\label{thm:main}
There is a fixed finite two-player Boolean constraint-system game $G_K$, with rational question probabilities, such that $\omega_q(G_K)=1$, no finite-dimensional strategy wins perfectly, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 D_{G_K}(\eps)=\eps^{-(3+\thetaK)/4+o(1)},\qquad
 \thetaK=\sum_{e\in K}4^{-(e+1)}.
\end{equation}
The exponent is Turing-equivalent to $K$. The sequence $\log D_{G_K}(2^{-n})/n$ has no computable certified rate of convergence.
\end{theorem}

The existence of increasing computable rational sequences with uncomputable limits is classical, beginning with Specker~\cite{Spe49}; this construction places such a limit inside the resource law of one fixed game. Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}--\ref{sec:game} prove its clock, compiler, finite-model and arbitrary-state estimates. Sections~\ref{sec:realization}--\ref{sec:heat} give the left-c.e. reader, operational extraction, heat and recovery laws. Section~\ref{sec:limits} proves the exact-trace obstruction and classifies matrix-approximable projection minima. Section~\ref{sec:gate} gives a complementary non-left-c.e. example, and Section~\ref{sec:classification} proves the full logarithmic classification.

\begin{theorem}[Power-scale classification]\label{thm:power-front}
For every $\Delta^0_2$ real $\beta\in(0,1]$, one fixed finite rational BCS game of value one, without a finite-dimensional perfect strategy, has matching power exponents
\[
 d_G^\to(\eps)=\eps^{-\beta+o(1)},\qquad \log D_G(\eps)=\eps^{-\beta+o(1)}.
\]
In $(0,1]$, the existing power exponents of log dimension, pure original-state entropy and single-copy entanglement of formation are exactly the $\Delta^0_2$ reals. The usable ceiling remains open; an exponent outside that class would force the polynomial formation/distillation gap of \cref{prop:power-gap}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{thm:power-realization}, proved in the power-scale part after Section~\ref{sec:classification}. Its new ingredients are the finite-moment source models, polynomial same-state clock localization, scaled counters and stopped normalized products. Its finite program computes a rational approximation while an older rational continues to emit multiplier pulses; no convergence modulus or witness Hilbert space is needed. Section~\ref{sec:problems} records the remaining usable ceiling and the question of exponents above one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dependency map}
The finite presentations, stop-or-continue proof, simultaneous upper models and matched resource estimates are proved here. The Boolean correspondence is used as stated from~\cite[Theorem~4.9]{DM-precision}, and the actual-state interface from~\cite[Proposition~6.1]{DM-precision}; Section~\ref{sec:usable} records how they feed an explicit extraction on the original mixed state. The main proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:realization} uses the carry-checker interface of \cref{thm:profile-interface} and the pulse construction of \cref{lem:profile-hold}. The second proof additionally uses the finite perfect-completeness reduction of $\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}$~\cite[Theorem~12.6]{JNVWY}. Both routes use Holevo's bound~\cite{Hol73} in the measurement count, and Devetak--Winter hashing~\cite{DW05}. Classical results used without proof are counter-machine universality~\cite{SS63}, real quantifier elimination and Shoenfield's limit lemma~\cite{Sho59}. The bounded tracial moment method~\cite{BK12,KMV22} enters only the exact-trace barrier of Section~\ref{sec:limits} and its uses in Proposition~\ref{prop:gate-gap} and Section~\ref{sec:classification-barriers}. The normal form~\cite[Theorem~5.1]{DJS13} enters only Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-range}. The real simulation~\cite{MMG09} and~\cite[Lemma~11.12]{DM-precision} enter only the remark on real strategies and question weights. The heat paper~\cite{DM-heat} is needed only for Section~\ref{sec:heat} and the heat consequences. These dependencies are substantive: this is not a self-contained proof of the imported correspondences or complexity theorem.


\begin{fronttheorem}[Exponent pairs and whole profiles]
The positive finite logarithmic dimension pairs are exactly $0<L\le U<\infty$ with $L\in\Sigma^0_2$ and $U\in\Pi^0_2$. One fixed rational BCS game realizes each pair with the same original-state usable pair. At the power scale every such pair in $(0,1]$ is realized, and these conditions classify dimension pairs there. A polynomial-delay pulse word with positive lower one-density and one-count $k$ gives
\[
 \log_2D_G(2^{-n})=\Lambda+\tfrac m2k(\Lambda)+O(\log n),
 \qquad\Lambda=\lfloor n/(2q)\rfloor,
\]
with the same usable profile, for fixed integers $m,q\ge1$. Under linear slope bounds, space-$o(n)$ target profiles are realized modulo $o(n)$, but some computable bounded-increment profiles with any admissible distinct endpoints are not realized by any game modulo $o(n)$.
\end{fronttheorem}
\noindent These assertions are proved in \cref{thm:profile-law,thm:profile-pairs,cor:profile-space,thm:profile-power-pairs,thm:profile-nonrealizable}. Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-time} supplies the necessary doubly exponential time bound. The arbitrary-game usable endpoint ceiling and the intervening profile frontier remain open.

The local lamp class has an additional sharp ceiling: $\log D_G(\eps)=O(\eps^{-1}\log(1/\eps))$, and its positive existing power spectra for log dimension, pure entropy and formation are exactly $\Delta^0_2\cap(0,1]$. General rational BCS games have no such upper-exponent ceiling: some fixed games have infinite upper power exponent for all three resources. Existing finite limits above one remain open. The usable-spectrum problem is reduced conditionally to weak pure rounding of original mixed strategies. For groups we prove effective upper classes and a trace obstruction for these counter presentations; neither supplies group realizations or excludes all analytic decoders. Turing-machine encodings into group rings and irrational analytic kernel projections are established precedents~\cite{Gra14,Aus13,PSZ15}. See \cref{thm:local-lamp-ceiling,thm:unbounded-power,sec:weak-rounding,sec:group-profiles}.

\subsection{Related work and credit}
Mousavi, Nezhadi and Yuen prove that deciding whether the quantum value is exactly one is $\Pi^0_2$-complete~\cite{MNY20,MNY22}. Their theorem rules out uniform computable dimension bounds. \Cref{thm:unbounded-power} extracts a stronger consequence for individual games: given any computable two-parameter envelope, one fixed value-one game exceeds each of its parameter slices infinitely often. The BCS version uses Paddock's SynchBCS construction~\cite[Section~4.3 and Proposition~4.22]{Pad22} and the weighted sequential-POVM proof in \cref{lem:synchronous-bcs-value}. This hierarchy argument establishes unbounded upper exponents, without establishing an existing limit above one. Theorems~\ref{thm:realization} and~\ref{thm:main} identify convergent coefficients for individual fixed games. The theorem $\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}$ puts halting information in a game's value~\cite{JNVWY}; it does not by itself identify a resource exponent of a fixed value-one game.

The algebraic connection between constraint games and operator assignments goes back to Cleve and Mittal~\cite{CM14} and, for linear systems, Cleve, Liu and Slofstra~\cite{CLS17}. Slofstra established an embedding theorem for solution groups~\cite{Slo20} and the non-closure of quantum correlations~\cite{Slo19}. Slofstra and Vidick introduced the hyperlinear profile as a quantitative bridge from approximate representations to dimension requirements~\cite{SV18}. Slofstra later obtained a finitely presented group with at least subexponential hyperlinear profile~\cite{Slo18}. Mančinska and Vidick give a two-player game with two questions per player, but answers of unbounded length, whose optimal value one is reached only in the limit: some local-dimension-$d$ strategies succeed with probability $1-O(d^{-c})$, and none exceeds $1-\Omega(d^{-2})$~\cite{MV15}. Ji, Leung and Vidick construct a fixed finite three-player game of value one for which near-perfect play requires at least $\Omega(\eps^{-c})$ qubits for a fixed $c>0$~\cite{JLV20}. Coladangelo constructs a fixed finite two-player score game for which every $\eps$-optimal strategy has local dimension $2^{\Omega(\eps^{-1/8})}$~\cite{Col20}. These results supply the framework and important growth examples; they do not identify the matched finite exponent in~\eqref{eq:main}.

Mehta, Slofstra and Zhao construct halting-dependent Pauli systems and embed them in finite presentations with polynomial bounds on relation decompositions~\cite[Proposition~4.8]{MSZ23}. Those bounds transfer lower estimates; they do not assert small simultaneous finite-model extensions. The construction here supplies its own upper models. The conjugacy gadgets used in our compiler are those of Fanizza, Kroell, Mehta, Paddock, Rochette, Slofstra and Zhao~\cite[Lemma~5.8 and Theorem~5.5]{FKMPRSZ25}. Their credit is essential: the passage from nested conjugacy to ordinary Boolean contexts is not a new gadget of this paper. We give its matrices and converse calculation because quantitative completeness is needed here.

The state-preserving Boolean correspondence was established in \emph{The price of precision}~\cite[Theorem~4.9]{DM-precision}; its linear-system predecessor and further dimension examples appear in the companion paper~\cite{DM-game}. We use that correspondence as an existing theorem. Section~\ref{sec:game} records the elementary square-root estimate needed for the present local-dimension application and checks the constants' effect on the exponent. Theorem~\ref{thm:realization} proves and strengthens the computable-coefficient inclusion conjectured in the discussion of~\cite[Problem~18.3(i)]{DM-precision}, for its original usable-entanglement demand as well as dimension. The $\Delta^0_2$ upper bound for dimension exponents is~\cite[Proposition~11.13]{DM-precision}; Lemma~\ref{lem:computability} records the rational case.

Uncomputable asymptotic data in finite systems have other precedents. Hochman and Meyerovitch characterize the entropies of multidimensional shifts of finite type as exactly the nonnegative right-c.e. reals~\cite{HM10}; their construction passes through a shift whose maximal symbol frequency is a prescribed right-c.e. real. Their theorem is a template for identifying the possible values of a physical quantity of a fixed finite system with a computability class. The exact precedent in form is Meyerovitch's entropy-dimension classification~\cite[Theorem~5.1(3), arXiv version]{Mey11}, for every $d\ge2$:
\begin{quote}
The class of entropy dimensions of $\mathbb Z^d$ SFT's is $[0,d]\cap\Delta_2$.
\end{quote}
The same theorem gives upper entropy dimensions $\Pi_3$ and lower entropy dimensions $\Sigma_2$. Our classification concerns nonlocal-game dimension exponents and matched entanglement coefficients. It is not the first $\Delta^0_2$ classification of an exponent. Sapir, Birget and Rips relate group isoperimetry and computation~\cite{SBR02}; a Dehn exponent is not a dimension exponent, and no identification between them is used here. Cubitt, Perez-Garcia and Wolf prove undecidability of the spectral gap over families of local Hamiltonians~\cite{CPGW22}. Bausch, Cubitt and Watson obtain uncomputable phase diagrams for a continuous one-parameter family~\cite{BCW21}. These are precedents for asymptotic noncomputability, rather than proofs of~\eqref{eq:main}.

The binary adding machine used below is the classical odometer on a Cantor set; its interpretation through operator algebras is standard~\cite{HSWZ13}. The new point needed here is a finite polynomial presentation and a carry estimate that remain quantitative in a non-flat state. The final information bound uses Holevo's theorem~\cite{Hol73} and the random-access-code principle exemplified by Nayak~\cite{Nay99}. Its branching estimate is proved explicitly.


Jockusch and Schupp prove that the densities of computable sets are exactly the $\Delta^0_2$ reals in $[0,1]$~\cite[Theorem~2.21]{JS12}, and Downey, Jockusch and Schupp classify the densities, lower densities and upper densities of computable and c.e. sets~\cite[Section~5]{DJS13}. The flag densities forced in Section~\ref{sec:classification} realize the same arithmetic class; what is new here is forcing such a density on every near-perfect strategy of one fixed game. The moving-window estimate in Section~\ref{sec:realization} uses the binary radical-inverse ordering of van der Corput~\cite{vdC35}, not a new discrepancy principle. Vidick's \emph{Almost synchronous quantum correlations}~\cite{Vid22} supplies the closely related spectral-compression mechanism; Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives the word-moment form needed here. Tracial moment optimization~\cite{BK12,KMV22} supplies the exact-trace lower hierarchy. Its distinction from general commuting-model NPA~\cite{NPA08} is the distinction used to escape the barrier in Section~\ref{sec:gate}.

Devetak--Winter hashing~\cite{DW05} is the operational last step in Section~\ref{sec:usable}. The new ingredient is the inactive-outcome extension of the triangular extraction in~\cite[Theorem~6.2]{DM-precision} and its cyclic finite-alphabet form~\cite[Lemma~6.3]{DM-precision}, with random targets and an original-mixed-state bridge. The heat conclusion invokes the existing reset sandwich of~\cite[Theorem~2.1 and Corollary~2.2]{DM-heat}. Kolchinsky and Wolpert~\cite{KW20} study noncomputable heat functions for universal Turing machines. Corollary~\ref{cor:heat} and Theorem~\ref{thm:gate-resources} identify uncomputable logarithmic precision coefficients for fixed finite nonlocal games and compare their local and joint reset schedules. Section~\ref{sec:gate} additionally uses the finite perfect-completeness form of Ji--Natarajan--Vidick--Wright--Yuen, states its exact theorem numbers, and credits the Connes embedding and commuting-complexity context, including Lin~\cite{Lin25}.

Fixed-size quantum-correlation undecidability also has important precedents in Fu--Miller--Slofstra~\cite{FMS21}, and quantitative spectral-gap and representation-stability methods for Pauli-testing game families in de la Salle~\cite{Salle24}. Their statements concern the specified correlation inputs or varying game families. They do not alone supply a matched fixed-game original-state usable spectrum. Finite-field independence and small-bias methods are credited to Naor--Naor and Alon--Goldreich--H\aa stad--Peralta~\cite{NN93,AGHP92} at the source construction.

The power-scale comparison with group isoperimetry is precise. Brady--Bridson~\cite{BB00} realize the dense constant-scaling Dehn exponents $2\log_2(2p/q)$. Brady--Bridson--Forester--Shankar~\cite[Theorem~A]{BBFS09} obtain $2\log_\lambda r$ from an irreducible nonnegative integer matrix with Perron eigenvalue $\lambda>1$ and rational $r$ above every row sum. Sapir--Birget--Rips~\cite{SBR02} program Dehn exponents under explicit digit-computation time hypotheses (double-exponential digit computation suffices for their stated $\alpha\ge4$ Dehn laws, and a triple-exponential necessary bound is proved); this is not an unrestricted theorem for all computable exponents. Here the invariant is an original-state game resource, with no Dehn-to-precision reduction. Arbitrarily slow rational computation is interleaved with continued pulse generation, and scaled-counter errors remain polynomial in physical depth. Meyerovitch's limit-computable entropy-dimension theorem remains the direct precedent in form. Classical counter universality is credited to Shepherdson--Sturgis and Minsky~\cite{SS63,Min61}; rotation coding and logarithmic independence to Morse--Hedlund and Baker~\cite{MH40,Bak66}. Clifford overlapping qubits~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{CRSV17}, the collective Houghton clock~\cite[Proposition~13.9]{DM-precision}, the source certificate~\cite[Definition~7.1]{DM-game} and the original-state extraction~\cite[Theorems~6.4 and~12.4]{DM-precision} are the essential geometric and operational inputs. Their role is explicit in the power-scale proofs.

\subsection{Proof outline}
The main route starts with two compulsory carry-checker clocks. A polynomial-delay counter program emits a pulse word of positive lower one-density, and stopped calibrators force the source-clock depth at a precision-dependent scale. If $k(J)$ is the one-count, \cref{thm:profile-law} gives the whole profile
\[
 \log_2D_G(2^{-n})=\Lambda+\frac m2k(\Lambda)+O(\log n),
 \qquad\Lambda=\lfloor n/(2q)\rfloor,
\]
with the same original-state usable law. Computable pulse densities realize the admissible lower and upper endpoint pairs by \cref{thm:profile-pairs}; taking equal endpoints gives Theorem~\ref{thm:realization} through Corollary~\ref{cor:profile-no-witness}. No fixed observable is assigned the limiting coefficient. The next paragraphs describe the second proof and its additional exact-trace information.

At source precision $4^{-N}$, three compulsory clocks cost $3N-o(N)$ bits of logarithmic dimension. They hold an address, fuel and time. For $G_K$, a finite counter program recognizes halting inputs on separated base-four cylinders. Reset forces initialized computations at every finite fuel scale. Exact tracial models must flag every halting cylinder; compactness extends this to all weighted approximate models, without an effective modulus.

A conditional clock contributes $\theta_KN-o(N)$ further bits. Explicit finite histories are diagonal labels on the existing registers. A moving register of optional bits realizes the conditional clock in dimension $2^{\theta_NN+O(\log N)}$, with computable $\theta_N\uparrow\theta_K$. Source precision $4^{-N}$ becomes game loss of order $16^{-N}$, giving the denominator four.

For an arbitrary left-c.e. $\theta\in[0,1]$, an address reader accepts exactly the binary values below $\theta$. Radical-inverse window counts match its finite flag density. Extra compulsory clocks add to the numerator, and replacing polynomial residuals by fixed powers divides the exponent. These two operations cover every positive left-c.e. $\alpha$. Finally a coherent extraction with an inactive outcome converts the expected active count into distillable Bell pairs on the original mixed state. The cited heat sandwich then matches both reset laws.


The complementary construction reverses the flag rule: an unflagged decoded cylinder must carry a finite perfect witness for its halting game. A fixed counter transducer with two operator registers tests every relation of that witness's projection presentation, packed into three data generators. Nonhalting unflagged corners have zero weight in matrix-approximable traces, and moment compression transfers that bound to the actual strategy state. Finite perfect witnesses can be admitted so slowly that their combined logarithmic dimension is at most $\sqrt N$, while the unflagged density tends to $\theta_K$. The flag density is therefore $1-\theta_K$, giving exponent $(4-\theta_K)/4$. The same extraction and heat sandwich apply. The exact-trace minimum is strictly smaller, as the barrier forces it to be.

The stop-or-continue construction replaces monotone admission by a stopping parameter $R_*$. Each continuation has one loaded finite perfect witness; each stop runs a scalar verifier against the entire remaining approximation. A bad address can neither stop safely nor continue forever. For the upper bound, any fixed threshold below the limit needs only finitely many witnesses. Letting the threshold advance sufficiently slowly makes their logarithmic overhead sublinear. This handles every computable convergent approximation, without a variation bound. Within this mechanism, even though both quantum and commuting values equal one, the exact-versus-matrix-approximable trace gap associated with the failure of Connes' embedding conjecture changes the attainable exponent class beyond the exact-trace left-c.e. barrier. The carry-checker proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:profile-no-witness} reaches the same class with no exact-versus-approximable gap, so this gap belongs to the flag mechanism and is not necessary for the classification.
