\section*{How this work was done}\label{sec:methods}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How this work was done}

\paragraph{Roles.} This paper is the work of its two authors together with current frontier AI models from more than one
developer. The authors set the direction throughout: they chose the questions, fixed the scope and the standards of proof and
review, decided which lines to pursue and which to stop, and take full responsibility for every claim. The models did most of the
exploration, derivation, computation and drafting, in a sequence of short research sprints. The written construction was preceded by separate proofs of the finite clock, guarded increment, and obstructions to exact periodic verification. A refutation-first audit then expanded the weighted carry estimates, the joint measurement count, and the simultaneous finite-model bounds. Those written proofs are incorporated here.

\paragraph{Validation.} The evidence for the results is of two kinds at this stage: complete written proofs and computations by the
scripts in \texttt{anc/}, which the reader can check. The review served to find errors; it does not replace the proofs.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Proofs.} The new construction and its quantitative estimates are proved in the text and appendices. The Boolean correspondence is invoked from~\cite[Theorem~4.9]{DM-precision}, with its local-dimension precision ingredients recorded in Section~\ref{sec:game}. The conjugacy gadgets are credited in Section~\ref{sec:compiler}. Results used for comparison are cited.
\item \emph{Proof audit.} A written refutation-first audit read the construction and its preceding clock and guard proofs. It found no fatal step or required change to the defining relations. It expanded the polynomial translation bound, the absence of a product of digit-depth losses, the all-branch measurement estimate and the entropy decoder, and checked the finite models with a disclosed test program.
\item \emph{Computation.} The archived finite-model script builds the operators at $N=4,6,8$ with a fixed one-counter recognizer that halts exactly on even inputs. It records every defining relation, required commutation, domain and state condition, and the derived decoding and reset identities. Its defect tables are in \texttt{anc/audit/recorded/}. A separate script multiplies the two conjugacy gadgets on finite matrices. No inequality in these tests was violated. Finite checks support the proofs and do not replace them; the arbitrary-state lower bound rests on the written proof.
\item \emph{Independent proof check.} A second refutation-first proof check was made by a model of another developer. It wrote its findings on the construction before reading the audit above, and then compared the two. It found no false step and no counterexample. It identified three repairs, each with a proof, and all three are applied here. The orthogonality context of the compiler now gives the projection $E$ its own Boolean variable, defined by an exact AND context (Section~\ref{sec:compiler}). The list of joint relations in Lemma~\ref{lem:joint} now states explicitly the commutators between different compulsory clocks and between conditional and fuel or time measurements, and bounds them through the raw conjugated digits. The translation bound~\eqref{eq:translate} is now linear in the number of translations, and the compulsory measurements are fixed explicitly. The check also recorded the two structural facts in Remark~\ref{rem:guards}. Its two numerical scripts, for the gadgets, the selector identities and the orthogonality repair, are in \texttt{anc/crosscheck/}.
\item \emph{Independent computation.} A separately written program, built from the construction without the archived script or its output, evaluates the finite models of Section~\ref{sec:finite} at $N=4,6,8$. It stores each operator as a sum of weighted basis permutations and uses its own three-counter test program. It evaluates every defining relation of Sections~\ref{sec:clock} and~\ref{sec:source}, every required commutation and domain condition, and the identities~\eqref{eq:forced-decoding}: $911$, $915$ and $919$ relations. It computes operator norms, by exact block decomposition, as well as flat norms. Every relation stated to be exact has defect zero. The same ten relations as in the archived script have nonzero defects. The nine that do not depend on the program have the same flat ratios, and the conditional-tail ratio agrees at $N=4,8$, where the two test programs have the same $K_N$. The operator-norm defects are $3\cdot4^{-N}$ for the clocks, $\sqrt2\,4^{-N}$ for the guarded increments, $4^{-N}$ for the carry step and at most $0.012\cdot4^{-N}$ for~\eqref{eq:conditional-tail}, and the dimension is exactly $2^{3N+k_N}$. Its code and recorded output are in the repository directory \texttt{verify/}. Like the archived script, it tests the finite models, not the arbitrary-state lower bound or the compiled game.
\end{itemize}

A Lean formalization of the version 1.0.0 core is in progress and is not complete. No completed Lean verification is claimed for this paper. The proofs, computations and review are offered for checking as they stand.

\paragraph{Version 2.0.0.} The new results came from the same process of model-assisted research and drafting. A refutation-first spectrum audit expanded the reader induction, zero-tail histories, moving-window count, residual-power precision, and the tracial moment and compression arguments. A separate refutation-first usable-entanglement audit checked the inactive-outcome protocol, its original-mixed-state bridge and its operational rate. An independent usable-entanglement check by a model of another developer re-derived those steps and supplied sharper constants. An independent spectrum check by a model of another developer also found no false step; its clarifications on early halting, monotone approximations, domain encoding, arithmetic and synchronous hardness are incorporated. These expansions are in Sections~\ref{sec:realization}--\ref{sec:limits}. The programs and their recorded runs are in \texttt{anc/spectrum/} and \texttt{anc/extraction/}. They test finite reader models, window counts, compression moments and Jensen, and the actual extraction protocol, including noncommuting controls and mixed inputs. They do not prove the lower bounds. The ancillary README records the commands, archived outputs and limitations of these checks.




\paragraph{Complementary construction.} Section 15 was checked by a refutation-first audit and by an independent check by a model of another developer. The latter required one repair, the use of separate projection families for the two players, which is incorporated here. The checks covered the tester induction, its non-self-adjoint register initialization, corner approximation, simultaneous finite models and the address-wraparound gauge. The original-state bridge and extraction proof apply to the new presentation, yielding its usable-entanglement and heat laws in Theorem~\ref{thm:gate-resources}. The exact external MIP*=RE theorem statements are cited there. The ancillary README specifies the commands, archived evidence and scope of the complementary-construction checks.

\paragraph{Priority.} For each headline result, bounded searches of arXiv and the general literature compared the statements with the closest work; the credits they found are given at the results. They found no exact preemption of the composed fixed-game exponent, which is not an exhaustive priority verdict. The search included dimension witnesses and hyperlinear profiles, noncomputable exponents, symbolic entropy and frequency realization, computability and asymptotic density, radical-inverse discrepancy, almost synchronous compression, tracial moment hierarchies, one-way hashing, bounded polynomial and Boolean encodings, Cantor adding machines, and computation in finite presentations. The conjugacy gadgets are credited to Fanizza and coauthors; the measurement information bound to Holevo and the random-access-code literature; the operator-assignment interpretation to the constraint-game literature; and the state-preserving Boolean correspondence to the cited companion work. The finite presentation, its simultaneous models, and the matched same-state estimates are the specific construction proved here. For version 2.0.0 the searches also covered realization of computability classes as resource exponents, the MIP*=RE witness and commuting-value literature, and synchronous approximation.
